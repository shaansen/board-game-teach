Learn it and play it · 17 points

The Castles ofBurgundy

A hex board in front of you, thirty-seven empty spaces on it, and two dice a turn for twenty-five turns. This page teaches you what the game is and gives you the exact rules, so you can play it straight from here.

How to read this page

Every point explains one idea in plain words, then gives you a box like this one with the actual rule: the real numbers, the real limits, the things people get wrong. Read just the essay to understand the game. Read the boxes too and you can teach it at the table.

Everything here is the base game for 2 to 4 players, using estate board number 1, which is the board the rules tell you to start on.

Rules from the alea / Ravensburger English rulebook

1 The big picture

You are filling in a map, and every tile you lay pays you twice.

Castles of Burgundy is a fifteenth century Loire valley in the fiction and a beautifully tight efficiency puzzle in practice. In front of you is your estate: a honeycomb of thirty-seven hex spaces, grouped into coloured regions. Rivers are blue, cities are beige, pastures are light green, and so on. Each space also carries a number from 1 to 6.

You take tiles from a shared board and lay them into that honeycomb. Two things then happen, and understanding both is understanding the game. First, the tile does something the moment it lands: a ship fetches you goods, a pasture pays out points, a building does a favour. Second, when a tile finishes off the last empty space of its coloured region, the whole region scores, and it scores far more than the individual tiles ever did.

So a good turn is never just "I placed a thing". It is "I placed a thing, it did a job on the way in, and it brought a region one space closer to paying out." Nobody attacks you and nobody blocks your board. The competition is for the tiles on the shared board, for turn order, and for the bonuses that go to whoever finishes a colour first.

The actual rule: what you are doing
  • The game lasts 5 phases (A to E). Each phase is 5 rounds. That is 25 turns each, and since every turn is two dice, 50 dice actions for the whole game.
  • You score on a victory point track running 0 to 100. Pass 100 and you flip your victory point tile to its 100 side; pass 200 and you flip it again.
  • Most victory points wins.
  • Tie: the tied player with the fewest empty spaces in their estate wins. Still tied, the one who went later in turn order wins.
Rulebook: game idea, game end
Remember this

Every tile pays twice: once when it lands, once when it completes its region. The second payment is the bigger one.

2 The two boards

One board is the shop and the clock. The other one is you.

The board in the middle of the table is not a map and nothing moves across it. It is a shop: six numbered depots, each holding a few face-up tiles, and each carrying a stack of goods. The number on the depot is the die face you need to buy from it. Around the edge it also keeps the time, the turn order and the score.

Your own board is the estate, plus a small clerical corner: three spaces where tiles wait before you place them, spaces for your goods, your money and your workers, and a spot to park dice you have already spent. Nothing in that corner scores. It is all working space.

The actual rule: what is on each board

The game board has 5 phase spaces (A to E), 5 round spaces, 6 numbered depots (1 to 6), each with four spaces for six-sided tiles and one large goods space, 1 central black depot, 12 spaces for bonus tiles, a turn order track and a victory point track from 0 to 100.

Your player board has the estate (37 spaces on board number 1, split into coloured regions), 3 storage spaces for six-sided tiles, 3 goods storage spaces, a space for sold goods, silverling storage, worker storage, used die storage, and printed summaries of the tiles and actions.

There are 6 double-sided player boards: board number 1 appears four times, boards 2 to 9 once each. Play your first game on number 1, the side the rules point you at.

Rulebook: game setup, the player boards
Remember this

Depot numbers are die faces. Estate space numbers are die faces. The entire game is the numbers 1 to 6, used two different ways.

3 Setting up

Ten minutes the first time, three minutes ever after.

The fiddliest part is sorting one hundred and sixty-four little hex tiles by the colour of their backs, not their fronts. Do it once into six bags or bowls and setup is quick forever after.

The actual rule: setup
  1. Put the game board in the middle. Put the 20 silverlings, the 30 worker tiles and the white die beside it.
  2. Sort the 164 six-sided tiles by the colour of their backs into separate face-down supplies beside the board.
  3. Shuffle the 42 goods tiles face down. Make 5 piles of 5 and put one on each phase space A to E. The remaining 17 stay in a face-down supply.
  4. Put the 12 bonus tiles on their spaces on the board (one large and one small in each of the six colours).
  5. Each player takes a player board, number 1 side up; 1 castle tile placed face up on the dark green middle space (the one showing a 6); 3 random goods tiles from the supply of 17, put face up on the three goods storage spaces, same colours stacked together; their 2 dice; a playing piece on the 0/100 space of the victory point track; 1 silverling; and their 100/200 victory point tile set aside.
  6. Roll to find a start player. The start player takes 1 worker, the next player clockwise 2, the third 3, the fourth 4.
  7. Everyone puts their second playing piece on the first space of the turn order track, stacked so the start player is on top and the last player is at the bottom.
  8. Give the start player the white die.
Rulebook: game setup
Remember this

Going last is not a punishment: the last player starts with four workers, and workers are the thing that turns a bad roll into the roll you wanted.

4 The clock

Five phases of five rounds, and the goods tiles count them down for you.

Each phase begins by wiping the shop and restocking it, so the tiles you left behind are gone. Then five rounds happen. In each round everybody rolls at the same time and then plays in turn order.

There is one lovely piece of design here. At the start of each round the start player rolls a white die and puts one goods tile onto the depot matching it. Five goods tiles are dealt to the phase at the start, one goes out each round, so the goods still waiting to be dealt tell you exactly how many rounds are left in the phase, and how much of the game remains. You never have to ask.

The actual rule: setting up a phase
  • Remove all six-sided tiles left on the board and return them to the box, not to the supply. (You may leave ships, mines and castles in place, since they are all identical anyway. Goods tiles on the depots stay put.)
  • Draw new tiles face up onto the matching-coloured spaces in the six numbered depots: in a 2-player game only the spaces marked 2 (12 spaces), in a 3-player game those marked 2 and 3 (18 spaces), in a 4-player game all 24.
  • Three-player exception: the dark green space in depot 6 gets a castle in phases A, C and E, but a mine in phases B and D.
  • Draw black-backed tiles face up onto the central black depot, between 2 and 8 of them depending on the number of players.
  • Take the 5 goods tiles for this phase and lay them face up on the five round spaces.
Rulebook: setup for each phase
The actual rule: a round
  • All players roll their two dice at the same time and leave them in view. The start player also rolls the white die.
  • The start player takes the topmost goods tile from the round spaces and puts it on the goods space of the depot matching the white die.
  • The white die is then finished. It cannot be used for an action and can never be adjusted with workers.
  • Players then take their turns in turn order: right to left along the turn order track, and top to bottom within a stack.
Rulebook: the five game rounds
Remember this

Everyone rolls at once, so plan while the others are playing. The goods tiles left on the round spaces are your countdown clock.

5 Your turn

Two dice, two actions, four things they can be.

A turn is over fast. You have two dice. Each die buys exactly one action, in whichever order you like, and doing the same thing twice is allowed. Then both dice go on the used die space and the next player goes.

There are only four actions in the whole game:

  • Take a tile from the depot matching your die, into your storage.
  • Place a tile from your storage onto the estate space matching your die.
  • Sell goods of the colour matching your die.
  • Take 2 workers, which any die can do, whatever it shows.

Read that list again and notice the tension it creates. A tile takes two dice to get onto your board: one to buy it, one to place it. Every tile you want costs you a whole turn, and there are only twenty-five of those.

The actual rule: a turn
  • A turn is exactly two actions, one per die. Any combination, any order, repeats allowed.
  • A used die goes on the used die storage space in the top right of your board.
  • Every action needs a die, with one exception: the "take workers" action ignores what the die shows, and buying from the black depot (point 9) uses money instead.
Rulebook: a player's turn, the actions
Remember this

Fifty dice actions, and a tile eats two of them. You will place roughly twenty tiles all game, so choose them like they cost something.

6 Workers

The dice tell you what you rolled. The workers tell you what you meant.

This is the rule that keeps a dice game from being a dice game. A worker tile turns one die up or down by one pip, and you can spend as many as you like on the same die. Rolled a 2 and needed a 5? Three workers. The wheel even wraps, so 6 goes to 1 for a single worker.

The catch is what workers cost. Getting two of them takes a whole die, which is half a turn, and at the end of the game two leftover workers are worth a single point. So workers are not a resource to save. They are a resource to spend the moment they buy you a placement you wanted.

The actual rule: workers
  • A worker tile may be used at any time during your turn to adjust one die result by 1, up or down. Used workers go back to the supply.
  • Going from 1 to 6, or 6 to 1, is allowed and costs one worker.
  • You may use several workers on the same die.
  • The take worker tiles action: use any die, whatever it shows, to take 2 workers from the supply.
  • You may never adjust the white die.
  • At the end of the game, every 2 leftover workers are worth 1 victory point.
Rulebook: worker tiles, the actions, game end
The trap

Sitting on eight workers at the end of the game means you spent two dice earning four points. Workers unspent are workers wasted.

Remember this

Workers are how you stop apologising for your dice. Spend them freely; the only bad worker is the one still on your board at the end.

7 Taking a tile

Three storage spaces, and a full shelf is a real problem.

A die matching a depot number lets you take any one tile sitting in that depot. It does not go into your estate. It goes onto one of the three storage spaces in the bottom left of your board, where it waits until a future die matches the space you want to put it on.

Three spaces sounds like plenty and it is not. If all three are full and you take a fourth tile, you must throw one of the old ones out of the game. Every discarded tile is half a turn you paid for and set on fire.

The actual rule: taking a tile
  • The die shows which numbered depot you may take from. Take any one tile from that depot.
  • It goes onto an empty storage space in the bottom left of your board. Never straight into the estate, even if you plan to place it with your other die this turn.
  • If all 3 storage spaces are full, you must first discard one tile from storage, removing it from the game, then put the new tile in that space.
  • Tiles left in storage at the end of the game are worth nothing.
Rulebook: action "take six-sided tile from the game board", additional rules
The trap

Hoovering up attractive tiles you have no space for. A tile is only useful if you can eventually roll its number, on a space of its colour, next to something you have already placed. Grab the tile you can place next turn, not the one you love.

Remember this

Storage is a waiting room with three chairs. Take tiles you can place soon, and take with the second die when you can, so you know what your first die already did.

8 Placing a tile

Number, colour, neighbour. All three, every single time.

Placing is the heart of the game and it has three locks on it. The die tells you which numbered space you may fill. The tile's colour must match that space's colour. And the space must touch at least one hex you have already filled, which at the start of the game means it must touch your starting castle.

That last lock is the one beginners underrate. Your estate grows outward like frost on a window, and if you build in one direction only, half the board becomes unreachable for a very long time. Once a tile is down it never moves again.

The actual rule: adding a tile to your estate
  • Take one tile from your storage. The die used shows which empty space of that number you may fill.
  • The tile's colour must match the colour of the space.
  • The space must be directly adjacent to at least one tile already placed. Your first tile can therefore only go on one of the six spaces around the start castle.
  • Tiles in your estate may never be moved or removed.
  • Whatever type of tile it is, its effect happens immediately after placing. Those effects are points 11 to 14.
Rulebook: action "add six-sided tile to your estate"
Remember this

Number, colour, neighbour. Grow in two directions early, or you will be holding perfect tiles with nowhere legal to put them.

9 The black depot

Two silverlings buys a tile without spending a die.

The black depot is the small central pool of tiles on the game board, and it is the only place in the game where money turns directly into board position. Once per turn, at any point in your turn, you may pay two silverlings and take a tile from it. It costs you no die at all.

That makes silverlings quietly powerful. Two coins are effectively half a turn of yours that you did not have to spend, and they let you fetch exactly the tile you need instead of hoping a depot offers it.

The actual rule: buying
  • Once per turn, before, between or after your two dice actions, you may return 2 silverlings to the supply and take one tile of your choice from the central black depot.
  • It goes to your storage spaces like any other tile, and the storage limit of 3 still applies.
  • The black depot is restocked with black-backed tiles at the start of every phase.
  • One yellow knowledge tile lets you buy from all seven depots instead of only the black one. Even then, still only one purchase per turn.
Rulebook: the central black depot
Remember this

A silverling is worth 1 point at the end and half a die in the middle. Spend them while they can still buy tiles.

10 Completing regions

This is where the points are, and they leak away every phase.

Fill every space of one coloured region and it scores at once, in two separate helpings. The first depends on how big the region is, and it grows viciously: one space is worth 1 point, but eight spaces are worth 36. The second depends on when you did it, and it shrinks: 10 points in phase A, dropping by two each phase, down to 2 in phase E.

Put those together and you get the real clock of the game. The same five-space region is worth 25 points finished in phase A and 17 points finished in phase E. Nothing else in Castles of Burgundy punishes dawdling the way this does. A region sitting one hex short is not "nearly done", it is losing you two points a phase.

The actual rule: completing a region
  • A coloured region is complete as soon as every one of its spaces is covered. It scores immediately, whatever its size.
  • Size bonus: 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28 or 36 victory points for a region of 1 to 8 spaces.
  • Phase bonus: additionally, the empty phase space on the board shows the current phase's bonus, 10 / 8 / 6 / 4 / 2 for phases A to E. Every completed region scores it, regardless of size.
  • Both are scored on the victory point track the moment the region is finished.
Rulebook: additional rules for placing six-sided tiles
The trap

Spreading tiles evenly over a beautiful, balanced, half-finished estate. An unfinished region has paid you almost nothing. Close the small ones early and the big ones before the phase bonus rots.

Remember this

Big regions are worth enormously more, and every region is worth less the longer you leave it. Finish things.

11 Ships

Blue tiles buy you goods and shove you up the turn order.

Placing a ship does two things at once, and the second one is the reason experienced players fight over them. First you take goods, which are your future money and points. Second you move one space to the right on the turn order track, and since you land on top of whoever is already there, you jump ahead of them.

Turn order matters more than it looks. The player who goes first sees the depots untouched, and the tile you desperately need is usually gone by the fourth player. A ship early in a phase can hand you the pick of the shop for the rest of it.

The actual rule: ships (blue)

When you add a ship to your estate, which can happen up to 6 times on board 1, both of these happen immediately:

  1. Take all the goods tiles on any one depot's goods space. Which depot is entirely your choice and has nothing to do with the die you used to place the ship.
  2. Move your piece one space to the right on the turn order track. If the space is occupied, put your piece on top of the others. If that makes you start player for the next round, take the white die.

Your goods storage holds at most 3 different colours. Same colours stack without limit; different colours need separate spaces. If you cannot store everything from the depot you chose, leave the rest behind.

Rulebook: ship (blue)
Remember this

Take the fattest goods space, not the one matching your die. And a ship is a queue jump, which in a game about a shared shop is worth real money.

12 Animals

A pasture pays for all its animals again every time you add to it.

Light green tiles show two to four animals of one kind. Place one and you score points equal to the animals on it. Here is the good part: if that pasture already holds animals of the same kind, every one of those tiles scores again as well.

So pastures reward concentration, not variety. Four cows into an empty pasture is 4 points. Four more cows into that same pasture is 4 plus the 3 already sitting there. Do it a third time and you are collecting for the whole herd all over again.

The actual rule: animals (light green)
  • Each animal tile shows 2 to 4 animals. When you place it, immediately score that many victory points.
  • If the pasture you placed into (a region of connected light green spaces) already contains tiles with the same animal type, score all of those tiles again too.
  • They must be in the same pasture, but they do not need to touch the new tile. Matching animals in a different pasture score nothing.
  • Up to 6 animal tiles fit on board 1.

Example from the rulebook: adding a 4-cow tile to a pasture holding a 3-cow tile scores 4 + 3 = 7. Adding a second 4-cow tile there later scores 4 + 4 + 3 = 11.

Rulebook: animal
Remember this

One species per pasture, as many tiles as you can fit. A mixed pasture throws away most of the points.

13 Buildings

Eight favours, each one usable once, and never two of a kind in one city.

Beige tiles are buildings and they are the flexible part of the game. Each one does a job the instant it is placed, and none of them needs a die to fire. The restriction that shapes everything is this: within one city, every building must be a different type. A five-space city needs five different buildings, so you cannot simply spam the one you like.

Two of them are the ones to watch. The city hall places another tile from your storage for free, of any colour, with all its effects, which can chain into yet another building. The carpenter's workshop fetches a building off the board without spending a die.

The actual rule: buildings (beige)
  • Placing a building triggers its advantage once, immediately. No die is needed to activate it.
  • Each of the 8 building types may appear only once per city, a city being one region of connected beige spaces. Estate 1 has 4 cities: one of size 1, one of size 5 and two of size 3.
  • The three fetching buildings (carpenter, church, market) take from the numbered depots only, never the black depot.
  • If the advantage cannot be used, for example there are no blue or green tiles left when you place a market, you may still place the building; the advantage is simply lost.
  • Up to 12 buildings fit on board 1.
Rulebook: building (beige), the buildings
Remember this

A city is a set, not a pile. Plan which eight buildings you need before you start filling one, and treat the city hall as a free extra placement.

14 Castles, mines and knowledge

An extra action, a wage packet, and a rule you get to bend.

The three remaining colours are small in number and large in effect.

Castles (dark green) hand you an extra action with a die of any value you choose. That is not a small thing: it is a third action in a game of two, and you get to pick the number, which means it is the perfect placement you could not otherwise reach.

Mines (grey) do nothing at all when placed. They pay one silverling each at the end of every phase, so the value of a mine is set entirely by when you build it. A mine placed in phase A pays five times. A mine placed in phase E pays once.

Knowledge (yellow) is the toy box: twenty-six different tiles, no two alike. Some change a rule for the rest of the game, some quietly score at the end. Read them when they turn up, because the ones that grant permanent die adjustments change how your whole engine runs.

The actual rule: castles, mines and knowledge
  • Castle (dark green), up to 3 times on board 1: on placing, immediately take one additional action of your choice, as if you had an extra die showing any result you wish.
  • Mine (grey), up to 3 times: no effect when placed. At the end of each phase, take 1 silverling per mine in your estate.
  • Knowledge (yellow), up to 6 times: 26 different tiles. Broadly they do one of these:

Remember this

Castles are extra turns, so take them early. Mines are a salary, so hire them early too. A phase E mine is one silverling and a wasted space.

15 Goods and selling

Goods arrive by ship and leave in one lump, whether you like it or not.

Goods tiles come in six colours and pile up in your goods storage, which holds three colours at most. Selling is one of your four actions: the die shows which colour goes, and all of that colour must go. You cannot hold a couple back for later.

Each sale pays exactly one silverling no matter how many tiles you sold, plus points per tile. That shape tells you how to play it: sell a colour when the stack is tall, because the silverling is a flat fee and the points are per tile. Selling one lonely tile is a whole die for one coin and a couple of points.

The actual rule: selling goods
  • The die used shows which goods colour you sell. You must sell every tile of that colour in your goods storage.
  • Sold tiles go face down onto the sold goods space on your board. You may look at them at any time.
  • You receive exactly 1 silverling per sale, however many tiles it was.
  • You also receive 2 / 3 / 4 victory points per tile in a 2 / 3 / 4 player game.
  • A warehouse building performs one sale for free, with no die.
  • Unsold goods left at the end of the game are worth 1 victory point each.
Rulebook: action "sell goods", game end
Remember this

Selling is all or nothing, so let a colour stack up before you spend a die on it. In a four-player game a stack of four is 16 points from one action.

16 Bonuses and the final count

Being first to finish a colour pays, and being second still pays.

Alongside the region bonuses there is a race nobody mentions in your first game. The first player to cover every space of one colour anywhere in their whole estate, all three mines, say, or all six animals, takes the large bonus tile for that colour. The second player to manage it takes the small one. Third and fourth get nothing at all.

Grey and dark green are the easy ones, being only three spaces each, so they usually go first and go fast. If you are three phases in and still on your first mine, that race is over and you should stop looking at it.

The actual rule: colour bonus tiles
  • The first player to cover all spaces of one colour in their entire estate takes the large bonus tile of that colour and scores 5 / 6 / 7 VP with 2 / 3 / 4 players.
  • The second player to do it takes the small tile and scores 2 / 3 / 4 VP. Third and fourth score nothing.
Rulebook: additional rules for placing six-sided tiles
The actual rule: final scoring

The game ends when the fifth phase finishes. Add:

  • 1 VP per unsold goods tile
  • 1 VP per remaining silverling
  • 1 VP per two remaining worker tiles
  • whatever your yellow knowledge tiles say

Six-sided tiles still sitting in your storage spaces score nothing.

Most points wins. Tie: fewest empty estate spaces. Still tied: later in turn order.

Rulebook: game end
Remember this

The last phase is for converting. Sell everything, place everything, and leave nothing in storage, because storage is the one place that scores zero.

17 Your first game

How not to lose it.

Everything in Castles of Burgundy is legal, mild and reasonable, which is exactly why beginners lose by forty points without ever noticing where it went. Five habits fix most of it.

1. Finish regions, do not decorate the estate

A half-filled estate is worth almost nothing. Pick two or three regions, close them, then start the next. The size bonus is steeply curved and the phase bonus is draining away at two points a phase.

2. Build early, score late is the wrong way round

Mines, castles and knowledge tiles are engines, and an engine bought in phase D barely runs. Anything that pays out repeatedly should be on your board in phases A and B.

3. Never let storage clog

Three tiles, and each needs a specific number on a specific colour next to something you own. If you cannot name the space a tile is going to, do not take it.

4. Spend the workers and spend the silverlings

Two workers is one point at the end, and one silverling is one point. Mid-game, two silverlings is a whole tile and three workers is any die face you want. Arriving at the end with a fat purse means you played the last hour poorer than you needed to.

5. Watch the turn order track

Four players all want the same tile out of the same depot, and the last player gets what is left. Ships move you up. If you are permanently last, a ship is worth more to you than the tile you were saving your die for.

Remember this

Close regions, build engines early, keep storage moving, spend what you hold, and buy your way up the queue. Efficiency is the entire game; nobody is coming to stop you.
