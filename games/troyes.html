A Chronicle Written In Play

Troyes

The tale of a rising family, told one lesson at a time
Your Cathedral, Stone by Stone
1
The Pitch & The Goal

It's the year 1200. You're the head of a rich, ambitious family in the French city of Troyes. You're not a king — but you've got money, connections, and big plans. The whole city has one massive shared project going on: building a giant cathedral that's going to take literally 400 years to finish. You won't see it done. Your great-great-great-grandkids might.

But you're not just watching from the sidelines. You're going to hire people, run little shops, fight off invaders when trouble shows up, and throw your weight around the city to boost your family's reputation.

Here's the twist that makes it spicy: every player has a secret judge. You each secretly draw a card representing a famous historical figure, and that figure has their own opinion about what makes a family "great" — maybe they only care about how rich you are, maybe they only care about how many soldiers you employ. Nobody knows anyone else's judge until the very end. Part of the game is guessing what your rivals are being judged on, while hiding your own.

Whoever racks up the most Fame (victory points) by the end wins.
2
Three Colors, Three Buildings, Three Kinds of Workers

Your city has three big buildings, and each one is run by a different "team" of citizens:

The Palace — your soldiers. Color: red.
The Bishopric — your religious folks, monks and clergy. Color: white.
City Hall — your everyday working people, craftsmen and merchants. Color: yellow.

Every citizen you place into one of these buildings becomes a tiny "worker" who, every single round, hands you a die in that building's color. Got 3 people in the Palace? You roll 3 red dice that round. Got 1 person in the Bishopric? You roll 1 white die.

Your citizens aren't just sitting there looking important — they're literally generating your resources for you, round after round, just by being stationed in the right building.
3
Dice Are Money — And Everyone Can Steal-Buy Yours

Here's the twist that makes this game feel alive instead of just "roll dice, do stuff."

When you roll your dice, they don't disappear into your pocket. They sit out in the open, right in front of you, in your own little zone of the city square — visible to every single player.

And here's the wild part: anyone at the table can use your dice. Not steal them for free — they have to pay you for them. But you can't say no. If someone offers to buy a die sitting in your zone, the deal happens, whether you like it or not.

So the real currency of this game isn't gold coins — it's dice. Coins and influence points are just side tools. The dice are the actual fuel that makes everything happen.

A quiet fifth "player" — the gray neutral citizens — also rolls dice into their own zone each round. Anyone can buy those too, paying the bank instead of a person.
4
Setup — Building Your Family From Scratch

Before the game starts, everyone places their very first citizens into the three buildings together — not each player doing their own thing, but taking turns one citizen at a time. The direction actually flips back and forth: first lap clockwise, next lap counterclockwise, like a little dance around the table. Don't rush this — where you put your first few people basically decides what your engine looks like for the whole game.

At the same time, everyone secretly draws that mystery judge — the Character card — and keeps it hidden. No peeking, no comparing.

The length of the whole game is secretly locked in too: a set number of Event cards go into a deck at setup, and the game ends the moment that deck runs dry. You'll feel the ending creeping closer as the pile shrinks.
5
The Shape of a Round

Every round follows the same daily rhythm for your whole city:

1. New shops open (first 3 rounds only)
2. Payday — income and wages
3. Everyone rolls dice — your workforce shows up
4. Trouble arrives — new events, maybe invaders
5. Everyone takes turns acting — the real meat of the round
6. Cleanup, then the next round begins

Don't stress about memorizing this — it's just the map. We'll walk each stop one at a time.
6
Rolling Dice — Your Workforce Shows Up

Remember those citizens sitting in your buildings? Every round, each one hands you a die in their building's color, and all your dice land together in your own zone of the city square, showing whatever number they rolled.

The neutral gray citizens also roll dice into their own zone — dice that belong to nobody, so anyone can buy them from "the bank" instead of from a rival.
7
Payday — Income and Wages

Before anything else happens each round, you collect a flat paycheck. Nice and simple.

Then it's time to pay your people. Your Palace soldiers and Bishopric clergy need to be paid a wage every round, or your reputation takes a hit. Your City Hall workers, though, are completely free to keep around — no salary at all.

Can't afford to pay everyone? No debt — you just pay what you can and take a small ding to your score. Loading up on soldiers gets you red dice, but it's the priciest team to keep happy.
8
Trouble at the Gates — Events and Invaders

Every round, danger shows up in the form of two new Event cards. Some events are just annoying — bad harvests, unrest, you read the effect right off the card. But some events are military attacks, and those hand out black dice — literal invaders knocking at your walls.

Here's how the city fights back: starting with whoever's holding the Start Player card, and going around, each player counters the toughest invader using dice from their own zone — the total value of the dice thrown has to match or beat the invader's strength. Your red soldiers count double against invaders — makes sense, they're the military!

Can't beat the invader you're facing? It leaves without a fight, but your reputation takes a small hit. Beat one, and you earn Influence — a resource we'll get to later.
9
Buying Dice — The Auction Never Really Ends

Now we hit the heart of the game: the Action phase. Going in turn order, each player either does one action or passes.

Almost every action needs a matching group of 1–3 same-colored dice — and those dice can come from anyone's zone, not just yours. But here's the twist that keeps everyone honest: the bigger the group of dice you're building, the more expensive each borrowed die gets. Grab just one foreign die and it's cheap. Build a group of three and each foreign die costs a lot more.

Assemble your whole group first, then go pay everyone at once — don't pay as you go, or you might overpay. Your own dice are always free. Buying from the gray zone means paying the bank instead of a rival.
10
Action — Activate a Shop

Imagine you own a shop in town — a bakery, a blacksmith, whatever — but your shop is empty until you hire someone to run it.

So the first time you want to use a card, you hire a tradesman: pay some coins, plop a citizen onto the card. That's your shopkeeper now. Once they're in place, you throw a group of matching-colored dice at the card to activate it — like feeding coins into a machine. The bigger your dice total, the more times the machine pays out.

Some cards pay off instantly. Others are slow-burn: instead of paying you right away, you bank little cubes on the card as saved-up favors, and later — during some other action — you can cash one in for a bonus. Only one favor per action, though; no doubling up.

Sneaky rule: once you hire that tradesman, you can't just walk away — you're required to actually use the card that same turn.
11
Action — Build the Cathedral

This is your family's contribution to that giant 400-year project. Using white dice (your clergy team), you place cubes onto numbered spots on the cathedral, matching the number you rolled.

Here's the catch: the cathedral is built in levels, bottom to top, and you can't place a cube on a higher level's spot unless the matching spot right below it is already filled. So a lucky high roll early on might not be usable yet if you haven't laid the groundwork first — literally.

Every cube you place earns you Fame and Influence right away, with the higher levels paying out a bit more Influence than the lower ones.
12
Action — Fight Back Against Events

Remember those Event cards menacing the city? You can spend a group of dice to actively work on beating one, placing cubes onto little banner slots on the card — like nailing up defenses one by one.

Whoever ends up with the most cubes on a card when it's fully covered gets the bigger reward; second place gets a smaller reward. Ties split the reward, rounded down — and if you're the only one who contributed, you scoop the whole thing.

Sneaky part: the neutral gray citizens count too — so "nobody" can genuinely block you out of first place. And if there's ever a tie for who takes the card itself, whoever placed a cube there first wins.
13
Action — Recruit, Farm, or Rest

Three more options round out your choices each turn:

Place a citizen in a building — unlike other actions, this only ever costs one die, no group needed. The number on that die decides exactly where your new citizen lands — and yes, that can bump someone else's citizen right out. One neat protection: if you've already got a citizen sitting in that "bumped out" spot, nobody can bump them further until the round ends.

Farm (Agriculture) — a simple, no-hassle way to turn yellow dice straight into coins. No shopkeeper needed.

Pass — stepping back doesn't mean going home empty-handed. Passing early earns you a small trickle of coins each time the turn comes back around for the rest of the round.

14
Influence, Wrapping Up, and Final Scoring

Influence is your "bend the rules a little" resource. Before countering an invader or taking your action, you can spend it to reroll a die, recruit an extra citizen on the cheap, or even flip a die over to its opposite face for a bigger swing.

At the end of each round, everyone collects leftover coins, dice get cleared away, and the Start Player card passes to the next person — then the whole cycle begins again.

When the game finally ends, it's time for the big reveal. On top of all the Fame you've already banked, you get bonus Fame for uncountered events you still hold, your shopkeepers' positions, and how much of the cathedral you helped build (empty levels actually cost you points!).

Then — the moment everyone's been waiting for — everybody reveals their secret judge. Each Character card scores every player, not just the one who held it. Whoever has the most Fame when the dust settles wins the game.
