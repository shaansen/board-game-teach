Learn it and play it · 14 points

The Taverns ofTiefenthal

You run a Bavarian pub. Every night the regulars stagger in, you roll for the staff who serve them, and you spend the takings before closing time. This page teaches you what the game is and gives you the exact rules, so you can play it straight from here.

How to read this page

Every point explains an idea in plain words, then gives you a box like this one with the actual rule: the real numbers, the real limits, the things people get wrong. Read just the essay to understand the game. Read the boxes too and you can teach it at the table.

Everything here is the basic game, Module 1, for 2 to 4 players, ages 12 and up. The other modules get a point of their own at the end.

Rules from the Schmidt Spiele English rulebook

1 The big picture

A deck builder where the cards are customers and the staff are dice.

Taverns of Tiefenthal is two familiar games bolted together, cleanly.

Half of it is deck building: you own a deck of cards, you flip cards off it each round to fill your pub with guests, and you spend the night's takings buying better cards that go back into that deck. Half of it is dice: the dice you draft each round are the staff, and a guest only pays when a die is put on them.

Your player board is a pub seen from above: tables where guests sit, a counter, a cashbox, a beer supplier, a barrel, a monk, and so on. Nearly every part of it can be upgraded by flipping the tile over. By round eight your grubby little pub is a machine, and that transformation is the actual pleasure of the game.

The actual rule: what you are doing
  • The game lasts exactly 8 rounds. The moon marker on the Monastery Board tracks which round you are in.
  • You win with victory points, printed in the top right corner of cards.
  • At the end, count the victory points on all the cards in your deck: both the face-down draw stack and the face-up discard stack. Most points wins.
  • Tie: whoever has more doubloons plus beer saved wins. Still tied, they share the win.
  • Two currencies: doubloons (money) and beer. Neither exists as a physical component. You count them in your head during your turn.
Rulebook: game overview, game end
Remember this

Guests come from your deck. Service comes from your dice. A guest pays only when a die is on them.

2 Setting up

Ten minutes of laying out, and then you never touch the box again.

Setup looks fussy the first time and takes two minutes once you have done it. The shape of it: a shared board in the middle with the shops around it, and an identical pub in front of each player.

The actual rule: setting up Module 1
  1. Put the Monastery Board in the middle, summer side up (the one without snow). Leave room around it, because you will be passing dice coasters about.
  2. Put the moon round marker above the first box of the round track. Put 3 counter guests per player beside the board; the rest go back in the box.
  3. Tavern cards: return all the Bards to the box (they are for Module 3). Sort the rest by type (Beer Merchant, Dishwasher, Waitress, Table, Beer Supplier) into an open display everyone can reach.
  4. Guest cards: find the 8 guests that cost 3 beer and stack them face up. Remove the 10 guest cards showing a white die at the top; they are for Module 3 and up. Shuffle the rest into a face-down pile, then deal 4 face up beside it as the shop.
  5. Noble cards go in a face-up stack to the right of the guests.
  6. Each player takes a tavern board and its 10 equipment tiles (tables, waitress, cashbox, monk, dishwasher, safe, host, barrel, beer supplier, beer storage), placed with the doubloon cost showing in the top left. Turn the host so its victory points are hidden.
  7. Each player puts their safe marker and beer storage marker on the "0" spaces.
  8. Each player takes a dice coaster and 4 white dice. Coloured dice go in a shared supply.
  9. Each player builds a starting deck: their 7 regulars in their colour plus 1 waitress, 1 table and 1 beer supplier from the shop. That is 10 cards. Shuffle it, face down, left of your tables.
  10. Each player puts their monastery marker on "0" on the monastery track.
  11. Whoever was most recently in a pub takes the beer mug and is start player.

Everything else stays in the box for your first game.

Rulebook: setup steps 1 to 12
Remember this

Your starting deck is ten cards and three of them are staff, not guests. That is why your first night earns so little.

3 The round

Seven phases, always the same order, and most of them are simultaneous.

The round never changes, which is why the game teaches itself by round three. Five of the seven phases happen at the same time for everybody, so there is very little sitting about waiting.

The actual rule: the seven phases
  • A. A new evening in the tavern (start player): move the moon one space along the round track and everyone takes the bonus shown there.
  • B. The tavern fills up (everyone at once): flip cards off your deck until every table is full.
  • C. Here comes the waitress (everyone at once): take one coloured die for each waitress you drew.
  • D. How can I serve you? (turn order): roll and draft the white dice.
  • E. Planning your actions (everyone at once): lay your dice on action spaces.
  • F. Time to serve the guests (turn order): actually take the actions and spend what you earn.
  • G. Closing time (everyone at once): discard the cards you drew, pass the mug, start again.
Rulebook: gameplay, phases A to G
Remember this

Cards first, dice second, spending last. If you are ever lost, ask which of the seven letters you are on.

4 Phase A

The moon moves, and it hands everybody a present.

Phase A takes five seconds and is easy to skip, which is a shame, because several rounds hand out free stuff and one of them hands out a free upgrade.

The actual rule: round track bonuses
  • Rounds 1, 4 and 6: every player takes a counter guest and puts it on a bar stool at the counter. Each is used once, then returned to the box.
  • A counter guest does one of two things, your choice: in phase B, after filling your tables, discard everything you drew and redo phase B from scratch. Or in phase F, discard it to move 1 space on the monastery track.
  • Round 2: take either a 3-beer guest card or a beer merchant card, face down on top of your deck.
  • Round 3: take either a coloured die (rolled, for this round) or a dishwasher card.
  • Round 7: take either a coloured die (rolled, for this round) or a beer merchant card.
  • Round 8: every player upgrades any one area for free. It works immediately, but you do not get a noble for it.

Anything bought or earned goes face down on top of your draw deck, so it arrives next round.

Rulebook: bonuses on the round track
Remember this

A counter guest is a re-roll for your whole opening hand. If your tables fill with rubbish, that is what it is for.

5 Phase B

Flip cards until every table is full, and you cannot stop early.

This is the deck-building half. You turn over the top card of your deck and put it where it belongs: guests sit at tables, staff line up beside the part of the pub they work in. Then you flip another. You keep going until every table has somebody at it.

Notice what that means: you do not choose how many cards you draw. A deck full of staff cards makes you flip deeper to fill your tables, which is good, because staff are the cards that make you money later.

The actual rule: filling the tavern
  • Reveal the top card of your deck and place it in the right spot. Repeat until all your tables are occupied, counting tables you drew this phase. Then stop.
  • Guests and regulars fill free tables left to right.
  • Nobles sit together. The first noble you draw takes a free table. Every later noble that round is stacked on top of that same one.
  • Drawn tables go to the right of the printed tables. Waitresses go left of the dog. Dishwashers go left of the dish washing area. Beer suppliers go right of the printed beer supplier. Beer merchants go right of the beer storage.
  • If your deck runs out mid-draw, shuffle your discard pile into a new deck and carry on.
  • Players finish this phase at different times. That is normal.
Rulebook: phase B, the tavern fills up
The trap

Stacked nobles look like a jackpot and are not. However many nobles you pile on that table, only the top one gets a die, so the rest earn nothing tonight.

Remember this

More tables means a deeper draw, which means more of your deck seen every night. Tables are how you get to use the cards you bought.

6 Phase C

Waitresses are extra dice, and three is the ceiling.

Every waitress card you drew this round hands you one die in your own colour, rolled straight away. A permanent waitress (the tile upgrade) gives you one as well. Those dice work exactly like the white ones, except you never pass them to anybody.

The actual rule: coloured dice
  • 1 coloured die per waitress card drawn this round, taken from the shared supply and rolled now.
  • Upgrading the waitress tile gives you 1 more every round, starting the round after you buy it.
  • Maximum 3 coloured dice at once. Extra waitresses beyond that give you nothing.
  • Coloured dice are yours to keep for the round. Used ones go back to the shared supply at the end of your turn.
Rulebook: phase C, here comes the waitress
Remember this

Four white dice is what everyone gets. Waitresses are how you get a fifth, sixth and seventh, and the cap is three extra.

7 Phase D

Roll four, keep one, pass the coaster left. Four times.

This is the mechanism people remember. Everybody rolls their own four white dice onto their own beer coaster at the same time. Then, in turn order, each player takes one die off their coaster and tucks it under their board. Everyone slides their coaster to the left, and you do it again with the three dice that arrive.

Four passes later, everybody has four white dice and nobody got exactly what they wanted.

The actual rule: the dice draft
  • All players roll their 4 white dice onto their own coaster at the same time, then set them down showing the rolled numbers.
  • Starting with the start player and going clockwise, each player takes 1 white die from the coaster in front of them and puts it under their tavern board.
  • When everyone has taken one, all coasters pass to the left at the same time.
  • Repeat 3 more times, until every coaster is empty and every player has 4 white dice.
  • Those 4 white dice plus any coloured dice from phase C are what you have to work with.
Rulebook: phase D, how can I serve you?
Remember this

Take what your guests need first. A high die is not automatically a good die: a guest wanting a "2" cannot be served with a "6".

8 Phase E

Lay the dice out before you spend anything. It is a rough draft.

Before you actually do anything, you park every die you own on the action space you intend to use it on. Nothing happens yet. This is a planning layout, and you are allowed to change your mind right up until you start removing dice.

Action spaces care about the number on the die. A guest card shows what it wants: some want a specific number, some show a question mark and accept anything. Get to know four spaces and you know the pub:

The actual rule: placing dice
  • A space showing a number of pips needs a die of exactly that value.
  • A space showing "?" takes a die of any value.
  • A green arrow with "1x" means only one die may go there. A green arrow with "..." means any number of dice may go there.
  • Even with several nobles stacked at one table, you may only place 1 die on the top noble's space.
  • Dishwashers: for each dishwasher card in your pub, one die may be placed on a space as if it were 1 higher. You do not turn the die, you leave the real number showing, so you can see how many dishwashers you used. Values do not wrap: a "6" can never become a "1". Several dishwashers can be stacked on the same die.
  • The permanent dishwasher tile gives you one such use per round, starting the round after you buy it.
  • This layout is provisional. You can rearrange freely until you start taking actions.
Rulebook: phase E, planning your actions
Remember this

Dishwashers are the "close enough" card. They turn a 4 into the 5 the monk wanted, and they are the cure for a bad roll.

9 Phase F

Take the dice off one at a time, and count in your head.

Now the money appears. In turn order, you pull your planned dice off their spaces one by one, and each one you remove pays you something. Doubloons and beer are imaginary: no coins, no tokens, just a running total you hold in your head until you spend it.

The order is completely up to you, and mixing is normal: earn a few doubloons, buy an upgrade that makes beer better, then earn the beer.

The actual rule: what each space pays
  • A guest, regular or noble: remove the die, get the doubloons printed at the bottom right of that card or space.
  • Cashbox: remove any die, get 1 doubloon. Upgraded, 3 doubloons.
  • Beer supplier: only dice showing 1 or 6 may go here. Each die removed pays 1 beer, plus 1 more per beer supplier card next to the space. Upgraded, the die itself pays 2 beer.
  • Beer merchant: each beer merchant card you drew in phase B pays exactly 1 beer. It does not boost the beer supplier.
  • Barrel (house brew): remove any die, get 1 beer. Upgraded, 2 beer.
  • Monk: only dice showing 5. Each one moves your monastery marker 1 space, or 2 if upgraded. Passing or landing on a bonus space gives you that bonus at once. Going past space 22 wraps back to the start of the track.
  • Remove each die as you use it. Used white dice go back on your coaster, coloured dice back to the supply.
  • Overshoot is fine: leftover doubloons and beer stay available for later actions this turn.
Rulebook: phase F, the action spaces explained
Remember this

Beer supplier eats 1s and 6s, the monk eats 5s. Those three numbers are why an "unwanted" die is often the best one on the coaster.

10 Spending doubloons

Money buys staff cards and upgrades, and upgrades hand you a noble.

Doubloons do two jobs. They buy tavern cards from the shared display, which go into your deck, and they flip the tiles on your board to their better side.

The upgrade rule that decides games: every upgrade gives you a free noble card. Nobles are victory points. New players forget this constantly, so say it out loud the first few times.

The actual rule: doubloon actions

Buying tavern cards. Take one or more cards from the display (beer merchant, dishwasher, waitress, table, beer supplier) and put them face down on top of your deck. Cost is printed top left. Limit: 1 card of each type per round.

Upgrading. Any area of your pub except the host counter can be upgraded by paying the doubloon cost in the banner top left of that area, then flipping the tile over.

  • You immediately take 1 noble from the supply, face down on top of your deck.
  • Beer supplier, cashbox, barrel, safe, beer storage and monk upgrades work immediately.
  • Waitress, dishwasher and table upgrades only start working next round.
  • Dice sitting on an upgraded tile go back onto it after the flip.

Special offer. You can make an upgrade cheaper by handing back matching tavern cards you drew this round: 3 doubloons off per dishwasher, 4 off per waitress, 5 off per table, 6 off per beer supplier. The price can never drop below 0, and you never earn money this way.

Rulebook: doubloon actions, special offer
The trap

Forgetting the free noble. If you upgraded and did not take a noble card, you skipped victory points that cost you nothing. The symbol above the host counter is printed there to remind you.

Remember this

An upgrade is two things at once: a permanently better pub and a free noble. That is why upgrading usually beats buying another card.

11 Spending beer

Beer hires people: one guest a night, or a fistful of nobles.

Beer buys the other half of your deck. You may recruit one guest from the display each round, and you can pay for nobles in bulk if you have had a very good night.

The actual rule: beer actions
  • Recruit a guest: take 1 guest card from the display, pay the beer cost printed top left, put it face down on top of your deck. Guests cost between 3 and 8 beer. Limit: 1 guest per round.
  • When a guest is taken, immediately refill the gap from the face-down guest deck.
  • Some guests show an immediate bonus in the middle of the card. You get it once, when you recruit them, and never again.
  • Recruit nobles: pay 9 beer for 1, 14 for 2, or 18 for 3. Face down on top of your deck. You may do this at any point in your turn.
Rulebook: beer actions, recruit nobles

Look at those noble prices for a second. Nine beer for one noble, but eighteen for three. A single enormous beer night is worth far more than three ordinary ones, which is the whole argument for stacking beer suppliers and upgrading the barrel.

Remember this

One guest per round, no exceptions, so make it the right one. Beer above that is best spent in one big lump on nobles.

12 Closing time

Save two, lose the rest, and the mug moves on.

The end of the night is where beginners quietly throw away half their earnings. Whatever you have not spent is almost entirely lost.

The actual rule: end of your turn and phase G
  • When you stop taking actions, you may put up to 2 doubloons in your safe and up to 2 beer in your beer storage. Upgraded, those limits become 5 each.
  • Everything above that is lost. So are any unused dice, which go back to the coaster or the supply.
  • Phase G: every player picks up the cards they placed in phase B and discards them face up beside their deck.
  • If the moon is on the last space of the round track, the game ends now.
  • Otherwise pass the beer mug clockwise and start again at phase A.
Rulebook: end of your action phase, phase G, game end
The trap

Ending on 8 beer when the guest you wanted costs 9. You keep 2 and burn 6. Before you remove your last dice, count what you can actually reach, and take the cheaper guest rather than the bigger pile of nothing.

Remember this

Spend down to 2 and 2 every single night. The safe is a pocket, not a bank.

13 The modules

The box is five games, and it wants you to play them in order.

What you have read so far is Module 1, the basic game. The box holds four more, each adding a layer, each meant to be added after you have played the one before.

The actual rule: what each module adds
  • Module 1: the basic game. Attract as many nobles as you can.
  • Module 2, schnapps: the monastery board flips to its winter side. Schnapps tokens buy bonus actions from entertainer tiles that arrive in rounds 2, 3 and 5.
  • Module 3, reputation: flip the host tile to its reputation track. A second route to beer and nobles, and the Bards and the white-die guest cards come back into the game.
  • Module 4: each player picks a starting card that sets their opening equipment, so the pubs stop being identical.
  • Module 5, the guest book: signature tiles come off the guests you recruit and fill a book of four columns for bonuses.

Each module needs all the ones before it: Module 4 means playing 1, 2 and 3 too. The rulebook recommends new players start with Module 1 alone, and says experienced gamers can start with 2 and 3 added.

Rulebook: the modules, supplement M1 to M4
Remember this

If your first game felt thin, that was the design working. The complexity is in the next envelope, and it is meant to arrive next week.

14 Your first game

How not to lose it: five habits from behind the bar.

1. Upgrade something almost every round

An upgrade is a better pub forever and a free noble in your deck. Two upgrades a game is a losing pace; five or six is a winning one, and round 8 gives you one free.

2. Buy tables early

Tables make you draw deeper, so you see more of your good cards every night. A pub with one extra table is quietly running a bigger deck than everybody else.

3. Put 1s and 6s to work

A "1" feels like a wasted die and it is beer supplier fuel. With two beer supplier cards beside the space, a single 1 pays three beer. That is how nine-beer nights happen.

4. Aim at one purchase per night

Decide what you are buying before you pull the first die, then earn exactly that. Ending with 8 beer and no plan wastes six of it.

5. Take the noble. Take the noble.

Every upgrade gives one. Nobles are the points. If you remember one rule from this page at the table, make it this one.

Remember this

Wide pub, deep draw, 1s and 6s into beer, one clear target a night, and never skip the free noble.
