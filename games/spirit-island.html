Spirit Island A cooperative game about being the thing the map warns you about.

01

You are not a person. You are what people are afraid of.

In almost every other board game about exploring a new land, you play the explorer. You sail in, chop down the forest, build a town, count your points, and win.

Spirit Island hands you the other side of that story.

You are a spirit — something ancient and enormous that has lived on this island since long before anyone showed up to give it a name. You might be a river that floods when it's angry. A shadow that hides inside flame. A mountain that remembers everything. A tangle of green that grows over stone until the stone gives up.

Then ships arrive. Little wooden pieces full of Invaders who look at your island and decide it's empty. They start exploring. Then building. Then burning.

You are what happens next.

That's the whole game: you and the other spirits looking at those ships and saying absolutely not.

  • You don't have a pawn Spirits don't walk around. You spread. You place little pieces of yourself — called presence — onto the island, and you can only affect places where some part of you already is. Getting bigger and reaching further is most of what you spend the game doing.
  • Nobody at the table is your opponent Spirit Island is fully cooperative. There is no winner and no loser among the players. Either the island holds, or the island falls — together. The thing you're playing against is the box: a stack of Invader cards that never gets tired and never makes a mistake.
  • Being strange is the point Every spirit breaks the rules in its own weird way. One drowns whole towns. One turns Invaders' nightmares against them. One is a swarm of insects that no one can quite kill. You're not picking a color — you're picking a personality.
