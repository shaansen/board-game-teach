Field guide · complete in twelve pieces

Spirit Island A cooperative game about being the thing the map warns you about.

12 of 12 awakened
01

You are not a person. You are what people are afraid of.

In almost every other board game about exploring a new land, you play the explorer. You sail in, chop down the forest, build a town, count your points, and win.

Spirit Island hands you the other side of that story.

You are a spirit — something ancient and enormous that has lived here since long before anyone showed up to name it. A river that floods when it's angry. A shadow that hides inside flame. A mountain that remembers. A tangle of green that grows over stone until the stone gives up.

Then ships arrive. Little wooden pieces full of Invaders who look at your island and decide it's empty. They explore. They build. They burn.

You are what happens next.
  • You don't have a pawnSpirits don't walk. You spread — you place pieces of yourself, called presence, onto the island, and you can only act where part of you already is.
  • Nobody at the table is your opponentFully cooperative. Either the island holds or it falls, together. Your enemy is a deck of cards that never gets tired and never misjudges.
  • Being strange is the pointOne spirit drowns towns. One weaponizes nightmares. One is a swarm of insects nothing can really kill. You're picking a personality, not a colour.
02

The Invaders don't think. They run on a machine — and the machine shows you its hand.

There is no evil mastermind. There is a small cardboard track with three slots on it, and a deck of cards that walks across it.

Every card names a terrain: Jungle, Wetland, Mountain, or Sand. Each turn a fresh card flips onto the leftmost slot, and every card already on the track slides one step right.

The three slots, left to right
SlotWhat happens in every land of that terrain
ExploreAdd 1 Explorer — but only if the land is on the coast or next to an existing Town or City. Invaders spread from where they already are.
BuildAdd a building. If that land has more Towns than Cities, they add a City. Otherwise a Town. This is how a lone scout becomes a fortress.
RavageBurn it. Damage is dealt to the land and to anyone living there.

Here is the beautiful part, and the reason this game is a puzzle instead of a slog: a card takes three turns to cross the track. The instant "Jungle" flips into Explore, you know jungles get built on next turn and burned the turn after.

You are always looking at the future. The pressure isn't surprise — it's that you can see exactly what's coming and you don't have enough power to stop all of it.

03

Fear is a weapon, and it's the fast way to win.

You do not have to kill every Invader on the island. You have to make them want to leave.

Whenever you destroy a building, you generate Fear: 1 for a Town, 2 for a City. Explorers are nobodies — they scare no one. Plenty of Power cards also generate Fear directly, no killing required.

Fear tokens pile up. Every 4 Fear per player earns a Fear Card, which gets resolved at the start of the next Invader Phase — usually something like "Invaders retreat" or "destroy an Explorer in every land with Dahan."

And as the Fear deck empties, the Terror Level climbs, and winning gets easier:

Terror levels — what "victory" means right now
LevelYou win when…
1Not a single Invader remains on the island. Brutal.
2No Cities remain.
3No Cities and no Towns remain.
4You win. Immediately. They're already sailing away.
Terror is not a side-quest. It is a second, quieter win condition running in parallel the entire game.
04

Blight is the island's health bar, and it eats you too.

When a land gets Ravaged, count the damage: Explorer 1, Town 2, City 3. If the total hits 2 or more, that land gains a Blight token — a black splotch of poisoned, ruined ground.

Blight is nastier than it looks, in three separate ways:

  • It hurts you personallyWhen Blight lands somewhere you have presence, you destroy 1 of your own presence there. The island's wounds are your wounds.
  • It spreadsAdd Blight to a land that's already Blighted and it cascades — 1 more Blight goes to an adjacent land of the players' choosing. One bad Ravage can domino.
  • The supply is the clockAll Blight comes from one shared pool. Drain it and the island becomes Blighted: flip the card over for a permanent new penalty, refill, and keep going. Drain it a second time and the game is over.

So Blight is simultaneously your health bar, a timer, and a punishment for letting one region rot while you defend another. Most losses start with someone saying "we'll deal with that jungle later."

05

The Dahan already live here, and they hit harder than you do.

Those little figures scattered across the island at setup aren't decoration. They're the Dahan — the people who have lived alongside you for generations, who know your name and leave you offerings.

You can't order them around like units. But most of your Powers can nudge them: gather them, move them, protect them, rile them up.

And during a Ravage, they don't just take it. Sequence in a Ravaged land:

What a Ravage actually resolves
StepDetail
1 — Invaders strikeTotal damage hits the land (2+ = Blight) and the Dahan at the same time. Each Dahan can absorb 2 damage before dying.
2 — Dahan strike backEvery Dahan still standing deals 2 damage to Invaders in that land. Two survivors will flatten a Town.

This unlocks one of the game's best moves: don't kill the Invaders yourself. Shove Invaders into a land full of Dahan and let the counterattack do the work. You are the storm; they are the spear.

06

Growth: every single turn, you choose how to get bigger.

Your turn opens with Growth, and it is the most important decision you make all game — because it's the only one that compounds.

Your spirit's board shows two to four Growth options. Pick one (a few spirits get to pick several). Each is a small bundle of things like:

  • ReclaimTake every Power card you've already played back into your hand. Without this you eventually run dry — but spending a turn on it means growing in no other way.
  • Add presencePlace a new piece of yourself onto the island, within a listed range of where you already are. This is how you reach a burning land you currently can't touch.
  • Gain a Power cardDraw four, keep one. Your deck is small; every card you take reshapes what you're capable of.
  • Gain energyStraightforward fuel, right now.

The classic beginner mistake is grabbing shiny new cards every turn and then having no presence anywhere and no energy to cast anything. Growth is a garden. Water it early.

07

Your presence tracks are your engine — and taking presence off the board turns it on.

Look at your spirit board. Your presence pieces start stacked on two printed tracks, and every time Growth pulls one off a track and onto the island, it uncovers the number underneath.

The two tracks
TrackWhat it gives you
EnergyHow much energy you collect at the start of every turn. Power cards cost energy to play.
Card playsHow many Power cards you're allowed to play in a single turn. Starts painfully low — often just one.

So growing isn't only about reaching further across the island. Every presence you place makes you permanently richer or permanently faster. Early turns feel weak on purpose; a spirit at full bloom in the late game is genuinely terrifying.

Also worth knowing: a cheap 0-energy card you can cast every turn often beats an expensive 4-energy card you cast twice all game. Cost is not the same as value.

08

Fast or Slow — the most important word printed on any card.

Every Power card has one of two labels in its corner, and it decides whether that card happens before the Invaders act or after.

1 · Spirit
Grow, collect energy, play your cards face downChoose Growth, take your income, pay for the Powers you want this turn.
2 · Fast
All Fast Powers resolveYour chance to prevent. Push Invaders out of a doomed land, defend against damage, kill a Town before it can burn anything.
3 · Invader
Fear cards → Ravage → Build → Explore → advance the trackThe machine takes its turn. Nothing you do can interrupt it once it starts.
4 · Slow
All Slow Powers resolveYour chance to punish. Destroy what they just built, clean up the wreckage, reposition for next turn.
5 · Time
Time passesPlayed cards go to your discard, damage clears, everything resets.
Fast stops a Ravage. Slow answers a Build. Playing the right one at the wrong moment is the single most common way new players waste a turn.
09

Elements: the combo system hiding in plain sight.

Along the top of every Power card sits a row of little symbols. There are eight, and they are the secret handshake between your cards.

SunMoonFireAirWaterEarthPlantAnimal

Two things read those symbols:

  • ThresholdsSome cards have a bonus line: "if you have 2 Fire and 1 Air, do this bigger thing instead." The symbols on every card you played this turn count together — so a junk card can be worth playing purely for the icon in its corner.
  • Innate PowersPrinted permanently on your spirit board. They cost nothing and can't be discarded. They just fire, at increasing strength, based on the elements you've assembled that turn. Some spirits live almost entirely off these.

This is the moment the game clicks for most people: you stop reading cards as "what does it do" and start reading them as "what does it do and what does it unlock."

10

Three ways to lose. Learn them, because they're the real timer.

Defeat conditions
EndingHow it happens
The island diesBlight must be added and the pool is empty on an already-Blighted island. The land is too poisoned to sustain anything.
Time runs outYou need to draw an Invader card and the deck is gone. The colonists simply won and you were too slow. This is the most common loss by far.
A spirit is destroyedAny spirit with zero presence left on the island is gone — and everyone loses with them. Nobody plays alone.

Notice what's missing from that list: there is no penalty for the Invaders "getting points" or "spreading too far" on its own. The clock is what kills you. Which means the real question every turn isn't "am I safe?" — it's "am I killing things fast enough?"

11

How to read the whole board in ten seconds.

Before you touch a card, run this scan. It'll make you look like you've played fifty games.

  • 1 — Read the Ravage slotWhich terrain burns this turn? Find every land of that terrain with Invaders in it. That's your emergency list.
  • 2 — Check each one for 2+ damageExplorer 1, Town 2, City 3. Anything hitting 2 makes Blight. Anything already Blighted will cascade. Those are the fires worth putting out.
  • 3 — Read the Build slotThat's next turn's problem, forming right now. A Town about to become a City is much cheaper to kill today.
  • 4 — Count DahanAny Ravage land with 2+ Dahan may defend itself. Any Invader you can shove into Dahan is a free kill.
  • 5 — Say it out loudGenuinely. Tell the table which land you're covering. Spirit Island is won by two players quietly covering the same jungle and losing the mountains — and lost the same way.

That's the whole rhythm: see the machine two turns ahead, spend Fast to prevent, spend Slow to set traps, and grow a little bigger every single turn.

Everything else in this game — the wild spirits, the scenarios, the adversary decks that make England or Sweden invade differently — is built on top of those twelve pieces. You know the game now. Go be terrifying.
